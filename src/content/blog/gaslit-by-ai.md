*Forty-five minutes. Four forms. One word: "human".*

All I wanted was to book a warranty repair. What I got was a masterclass in how to take a five-minute job and stretch it into three quarters of an hour of slowly losing my mind.

## 🧾 Round one and two: the forms

I'd already registered the product on their site. Name, address, model number, serial number, proof of purchase. All of it, sitting in their system.

Then I went to log the repair. The form asked for all of it again. Every field. I now know my serial number by heart, which is not a flex, it's a symptom.

Halfway through, the site decided this wasn't a job for a form after all: "Please use our chat service." Off I went, patience already somewhere around 60%.

## 💬 Round three: the bot

The first chat didn't survive for posterity, I was too annoyed to screenshot it. But the exchange was short. I asked to book a repair. It told me to go to the website. The website I was on. The website that had sent me to the chat. Somewhere, an infinite loop was achieving consciousness. 🤦‍♂️

So I opened a second chat, and this time I came armed with a screenshot finger.

No games this round. The bot asked why I was contacting them, and I went straight for the "Talk to human" option. Its reply: "While I connect you to a member of the team, please let us know the reason for your call today."

Promising! So I let them know the reason. The whole saga, in a paragraph: tried to book on the site, site says chat with us, bot says go to website, I need a human.

![Me explaining the whole thing in one paragraph](/gaslit-by-ai/chat-1.png)

The bot read my paragraph and responded with a quiz: "To confirm, would you like to check your manufacturer warranty or enquire about a care plan?"

Neither. That's not what I said. "Something Else."

![The quiz nobody asked for](/gaslit-by-ai/chat-2.png)

And then it happened. Word for word, character for character, identical: "While I connect you to a member of the team, please let us know the reason for your call today."

The reason. The one I'd already written out in full. Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, contempt.

Side note: it's a chat window. Nobody is calling anyone. Even the script doesn't know where it works.

So I asked the only question left worth asking: "Are you actually connecting to a human?"

"Thank you. We are connecting you to an advisor." Thank you! It thanked me for catching it. And then the best part: "Our current Estimated Wait Time is less than 1 minute."

![Sixty seconds away this whole time](/gaslit-by-ai/chat-3.png)

Less than one minute. The human was 60 seconds away the entire time. The bot wasn't a queue. It was an obstacle course in front of an open door.

## 🙋 Round four: the human

And here's the bit that really stings. The advisor was great. Sorted it quickly, no fuss.

After asking me for my details. Again. Fourth time. At this point my address is basically a catchphrase.

> Same advisor. Same resolution. If the bot had connected me straight away, the whole thing takes five minutes instead of 45.

## ⚙️ A bad bot isn't neutral

Here's the take, and it's not "chatbots are evil". I work with this technology every day. Built well, they're genuinely brilliant.

The take is that a crappy bot doesn't fail neutrally. It's not a slightly-worse version of no bot. It's actively worse than nothing, because it sits between your customer and the thing they need, and charges them time to get past it.

Nobody finishes a 45-minute loop thinking "well, at least they're innovating". They finish it writing a blog post with a hashtag in it.

## ✅ If you're putting a bot in front of customers

- **If it can't do the thing, don't ship it.** A bot that answers "book a repair" with "go to the website" is a search engine with worse manners.
- **"Human" means human.** If someone picks "talk to a human", connect them. Don't promise a connection and then run a quiz.
- **Read what the customer wrote.** I gave the full reason in one paragraph. The bot's follow-up proved it hadn't processed a word of it.
- **Never repeat yourself verbatim.** Asking the same question twice, identically, after it's been answered is how you end up in someone's blog post.
- **Stop asking for what you already know.** Four rounds of the same details is not a process, it's a hazing ritual.
- **Ask whether you need the chat at all.** The wait time for a human was under a minute. A working form, or a straight handoff, would have done this in one step.

## 🎯 The takeaway

Five minutes of work. Forty-five minutes of experience. The gap between those two numbers is the damage your bot is doing, and it doesn't show up on the dashboard.

Fix the journey first. Then decide if you need a bot at all. And if you do: teach it the word "human".
